What to Avoid
With Ceramic Coating

From Savvy at Shelby Ceramic Coating, VIC, with firsthand experience from 500+ vehicles coated across Melbourne.

The Direct Answer

The critical list: avoid automated car washes (the biggest killer), avoid waxing over the coating, avoid alkaline or solvent-based cleaning products, avoid driving through the rain for 48 hours after application, and never let bird droppings or tree sap sit in summer heat. These five things account for 90% of premature coating failures I see.

The Complete Avoidance Guide, Written For My Customers After Every Job

Every Shelby customer gets an aftercare briefing when they pick up their car. I'm putting the key points here so you can refer back, share with family members who might drive the coated car, and use it as a reference before booking anywhere (including with me).

During the First 48 Hours After Application: Absolute Rules

Avoid water: No rain, no car wash, no morning dew if possible. The coating is curing chemically during this window. Water contaminating the surface before full cure creates water spots that are extremely difficult to remove later. I always check the Melbourne forecast before booking jobs and recommend customers do the same.

Avoid parking under trees: Sap and bird droppings on freshly applied coating during the cure window can cause significant problems, the coating hasn't hardened enough to resist etching yet.

Avoid touching the painted surfaces: Fingerprints contain oils and acids that can interfere with the curing process on fresh coating.

Ongoing: What to Avoid for the Coating's Full Life

Automated car washes, absolutely avoid. I cannot stress this enough. The alkaline chemicals (pH 11–13) and rotating brushes in drive-through car washes are the single most reliable way to shorten coating life. This includes "touchless" automated washes, the chemical spray is still damaging. Hand wash only.

Dish soap or household detergents. Dish soap is alkaline and strips protective coatings (it's designed to). Never use it on a ceramic-coated car, even in a pinch. pH-neutral car wash soap is the only acceptable product for regular washing.

High-pH or solvent-based cleaning products on painted panels. Tar remover, heavy degreaser, strong wheel cleaner, keep these away from coated paint. If contamination occurs, rinse immediately with water.

Machine polishing without recoating. Machine polishing with abrasive compound removes ceramic coating. If you need paint correction after coating (new scratch from a car park, for example), the coating in that area must be removed, the paint corrected, and ceramic reapplied to that panel. A professional who doesn't tell you this upfront is not someone I'd trust with a ceramic-coated car.

Leaving bird droppings and tree sap in summer heat. Both are acidic and both etch through coating given enough time and heat. Melbourne in January can have surface temperatures of 70°C+ on a dark car in the sun. That accelerates the etching chemistry dramatically. Remove promptly, a quick spray of pH-neutral detailer and a microfibre wipe is all it takes.

Traditional wax. Doesn't bond to ceramic properly, reduces hydrophobic performance temporarily, and washes off in weeks. Use a ceramic SiO2 top-up spray instead.

Automatic ionic dryers at car washes. Even if you hand-wash and then use one of these to dry, the heat can cause water spots to etch more aggressively than air-drying with a microfibre. Dry with a clean, high-quality microfibre drying towel or a dedicated automotive air blower.

What's Fine That People Often Worry About

Rain is fine, actually beneficial, as the hydrophobic surface causes beading that carries contaminants off. Normal driving is fine. Petrol and diesel splashes: wipe off promptly with a microfibre but they won't damage the coating on contact. Bird droppings in cooler weather (under 20°C): you have more time to deal with them safely, 24–48 hours typically before etching begins. Summer temperatures dramatically shorten that window.

People Also Ask

Can I use a clay bar on ceramic coating? +
A gentle clay bar can be used periodically (every 12–18 months) to remove surface contamination that washing doesn't remove. Use a lubricant spray and very light pressure. A more aggressive alternative is a ceramic-compatible iron fallout spray (like CarPro Iron X), which chemically dissolves iron deposits without physical abrasion.
How long after ceramic coating can I wash my car? +
Wait a minimum of 48 hours after application before the first wash. Some coatings specify 7 days before the first full wash, check with whoever applied your coating. After that, you can wash as often as needed using the hand wash method with pH-neutral soap.
Can I park under a tree with a ceramic-coated car? +
After the 48-hour cure window, parking under trees is fine short-term but not ideal long-term. Sap and bird droppings are the concern, ceramic coating makes them easier to remove than from bare paint, but sustained acid exposure in summer heat can still etch the coating. Rinse the car more frequently if you park under trees regularly.

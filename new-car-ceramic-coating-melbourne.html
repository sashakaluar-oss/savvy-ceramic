Why New Car Ceramic Coating in Melbourne Makes Sense on Day One

Every week I speak to Melbourne car owners who picked up their new car, declined the dealer's paint protection, and called me. They made the right call. Here's why day-one ceramic coating makes more sense than waiting — or than accepting what the dealership offers.

New Cars Aren't as Protected as You Think

By the time a new car reaches a Melbourne dealership, it has been transported (often outdoors), washed multiple times, detailed by dealership staff, and possibly sat in external storage for weeks. The factory paint is already experiencing environmental exposure. More importantly, even the lightest washing introduces micro-scratches to the paint surface. The car that arrives at your driveway may already have swirl marks forming.

Ceramic coating applied before significant use seals the paint in the best condition it will ever be in. The preparation for a new car is minimal — light decontamination, IPA prep — because the paint is clean and correct. This makes new car jobs faster and sometimes slightly less expensive than older vehicles requiring correction.

The Dealer Paint Protection Decision

Every new car buyer in Melbourne faces this decision. The dealership offers paint protection as an add-on, typically $600–$2,500. My honest assessment:

Some dealer packages use genuine ceramic products applied by skilled technicians — and these are worth considering, though usually overpriced. Most dealer packages apply a basic polymer sealant in 20–30 minutes, branded as ceramic protection, at a significant markup. The result fades within 12–18 months regardless of the warranty certificate.

My recommendation: ask the dealer exactly what product they're applying (name and brand, not generic "ceramic coating"). If they can't or won't answer, decline the package and call me.
